Journey

Asymptora started with a decision, not a plan. Both of us were working in fields we'd built real careers in, medicine, criminal law, and both of us kept running into the same question: are we solving problems, or just applying what's already been decided? We'd always been drawn to genuine problem-solving, and the market around us, in both fields, had grown saturated with exactly the kind of work that wasn't that.

The turning point was our daughter's birth. It's the kind of moment that makes you ask what you actually want the next years to look like, not in theory, but for real. We talked. A lot. We read, researched, and argued the decision from every angle we could find, the way we argue about everything, because that's always been how we work: nothing gets decided quietly between us.

Technology wasn't obvious from the start. Java, even less so. But the more we studied it, the more it felt like the right match for how we think: a language with real history, that talks to nearly every machine, built with rigor, still evolving, still improving with every version. That's not just a technical description. It's close to how we see ourselves.

We tried different paths before landing on the same one. That's not a coincidence: it's what keeps happening when two people who like debating everything keep arriving, independently, at the same conclusion. What we found in doing this together wasn't just company. It's mutual support in study, in work, in the intellectual back-and-forth that makes an idea better, and, on the harder days, the kind of support that keeps you from walking away when you've genuinely thought about it. Healthy competition too. Neither of us wants to be the one who's behind.

That's also why the name. Asymptora comes from asymptote, the mathematical curve that continually approaches a line, getting closer without losing its direction. That's the standard we're holding ourselves to: not a fixed point of arrival, but a commitment to keep improving. Always in pursuit of excellence, on purpose.

We built one name to carry two individual portfolios: separate work, shared strength. What's here now is that work in progress: real projects, documented the way production systems are documented in the industry, a lab we operate ourselves, decisions made and written down with their trade-offs. Living that process, daily, is already the result. Entering the job market is the next step, not the starting line.

We don't expect that to change once we get there. Continuous, deliberate study doesn't stop at the first job. It's the job. And we're building toward a kind of engineering that matters more, not less, as AI changes what "coding" means: architecture, judgment, and the trade-offs that only come from reasoning through a real system, the part no tool does for you.
