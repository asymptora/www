Janaína

Backend engineer, extending into product and user experience.

Before software, Janaína spent years in healthcare management, including acting as incident commander during critical operational failures: reading a situation fast, structuring a response under pressure, keeping people safe while they got through the crisis. She still thinks about user experience the way she used to think about patient experience: not just what should happen, but where things actually go wrong for the person living through it.

That question, not just whether a system works but how it's experienced by whoever depends on it, shapes how she approaches software. Java is where she builds that thinking into real systems: reasoning about backend logic, data, and the contracts a system exposes to the people who rely on them, treating a working feature as the starting point for a better question: what does this look like from the other side?

She has a habit of mapping a problem before writing any code: sketching the paths a solution could take, asking what each one costs, whether there's a simpler way, a safer one, a more direct one. Recognizing patterns quickly and thinking in flowcharts isn't something she only does for software; it's how she works through any complex problem, from a logic puzzle to a process that needs to be redesigned.

At Asymptora, this becomes the extension from backend into product: translating a business problem into requirements and a model, building the interface that puts a system in front of a real user, and paying attention to what that user sees when something fails, not only when it works.
